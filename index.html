PetKit PURA MAX (T4) ESPHome install guide

Back up the stock firmware, extract the factory calibration, configure, flash ESPHome, and restore the stock firmware if needed. The serial steps run in this page: nothing is uploaded, everything stays between your browser and the litter box.

Experimental firmware, at your own risk. It fully replaces the stock firmware and drives the motors of a device your pet goes into. Opening the device and flashing it may void the warranty or damage it. Only go ahead if you are comfortable with electronics and ESPHome, and never leave a pet near the litter box while testing.

1. What you need

  • A USB to serial adapter with 3.3 V logic (CP2102, CH340, FT232…). Not a 5 V one.
  • Three wires and a soldering iron (or test probes held on the pads).
  • A screwdriver to open the device.
  • Chrome or Edge on a computer for the interactive steps (they use Web Serial; Safari and Firefox don't support it). The equivalent esptool commands are given for every step.
  • ESPHome to compile the firmware, and Home Assistant to use it (optional: it also works offline).
  • A known mass, ideally around 5 kg and known to 10 g, for the weight calibration.

This guide was written for the T4-MAIN-V2.3 main board; the photos show a V1.3, laid out the same way around the ESP32. Other revisions exist: check the labels on your board before wiring.

2. Open and wire

  1. Remove the pet, unplug the litter box, and empty the drum.
  2. Open the top housing to reach the main board, next to the display.
  3. Find the ESP32 serial header, right next to the ESP32 module, labelled GND, WTXD, WRXD and WTX1.

The three ESP32 serial pads are on the underside of the board, labelled WRXD, WTXD and GND: solder the wires there, or hold probes on them firmly.

Don't use the other serial header. The DEBUG header (GND RX TX DEBUG, next to the beeper) goes to the motor MCU (ISD), not to the ESP32: esptool then fails with Failed to connect. WTX1 is a debug output of the stock firmware and isn't needed.

AdapterBoard
GNDGND
RXWTXD (ESP32 transmit)
TXWRXD (ESP32 receive)
3V3 / 5Vnot connected

The board is powered by its own power adapter: powering it from the serial adapter's 3.3 V pin isn't enough.

3. Download mode and connection

  1. Plug the USB adapter into the computer. Close any serial monitor: only one program can use the port.
  2. Hold the OK button (next to the display; it is wired to the ESP32 boot pin, GPIO0), plug the litter box power adapter in, then release the button. The ESP32 starts in download mode: a serial monitor at 115200 baud would show waiting for download.
  3. Click Connect and choose the adapter's port (on a Mac, cu.usbserial-…; not the Bluetooth one).

4. Back up the stock firmware, twice

		
Read the whole 8 MB flash before anything else. This backup is the only way back to the stock firmware, and it holds the factory weight calibration of your unit. A 4 MB dump isn't enough: the calibration and the second firmware slot are past 4 MB.

		
Read it twice and compare the two dumps. Identical dumps prove the reads are good; a difference means a bad contact or a corrupted read. Each read takes about 13 minutes at 115200 baud. Don't let the stock firmware start between the two reads: it could change its saved data.

shasum -a 256 petkit-stock-a.bin petkit-stock-b.bin

5. Prepare secrets.yaml

		
The firmware reads its passwords and keys from secrets.yaml, next to petkit.yaml. Fill this form to generate it; the calibration lines from step 4 are added when available.

		

			
Nothing leaves your browser. This page has no server: what you type here, your dumps and your firmware are never uploaded or saved anywhere. The file is only created on your computer when you download it, and everything is gone when you close the tab.

		

		

			
			
			
			
			
			
			

				
			

			

			

		

		
The API key is asked by Home Assistant when adding the device. Keep this file private: it is listed in .gitignore.

	


	

		
6. Compile the firmware

		
Compiling needs ESPHome on your computer (or the ESPHome add-on of Home Assistant); a browser can't do it.

		
    
			
  1. Install ESPHome and get the project: 
    python -m pip install esphome
git clone https://github.com/Triskae/esphome-petkit-t4.git
cd esphome-petkit-t4
    2. 
			
  2. Put the secrets.yaml of step 5 in that folder.
    3. 
			
  3. Compile: 
    esphome compile petkit.yaml
    4. 
			
  4. The image to flash is .esphome/build/petkit-t4/build/firmware.factory.bin. Take the factory one: firmware.ota.bin is only for wireless updates.
    5. 
		

		
Run esphome config petkit.yaml first to check the configuration. From the ESPHome dashboard: Install → Manual download → Factory format.

	


	

		
7. Flash ESPHome

		
Put the device in download mode again if it was restarted (step 3), and connect.

			Same thing from the command line
			
esphome upload petkit.yaml --device /dev/cu.usbserial-XXXX

			
ESPHome writes the factory image at 0x0 at 460800 baud and verifies it. No erase is needed beforehand.

		

		
When it's done, unplug the litter box power adapter, disconnect the serial wires, and plug it back in.

	


	

		
8. First boot

		
    
			
  1. The device joins your Wi-Fi. If it can't, it opens the fallback access point of step 5; its display shows a QR code to join it.
    2. 
			
  2. In Home Assistant, the device is discovered by the ESPHome integration; enter the API key of step 5.
    3. 
			
  3. Check the diagnostic entities: MCU Handshake turns on within a few seconds, MCU Link Lost stays off, and the drum and flap positions are reported.
    4. 
			
  4. Calibrate the weight sensor with a known mass, as described in the README.
    5. 
			
  5. Close the housing, then try a Level with the cover closed and no pet around before relying on automatic cleaning.
    6. 
		

		
Next updates are wireless: esphome run petkit.yaml uses the OTA password of step 5, no need to open the device again.

	


	

		
9. Restore the stock firmware

		
Writing the 8 MB backup back brings the device to the exact state it was in, factory calibration and pairing included. Use the backup of this same unit, never another one's.

		
The restore hasn't been tried on a device yet.

			Same thing with esptool
			
python -m esptool --chip esp32 --port /dev/cu.usbserial-XXXX --baud 115200 --before no-reset --after no-reset-stub write-flash --flash-mode keep --flash-freq keep --flash-size keep 0x0 petkit-stock-a.bin
python -m esptool --chip esp32 --port /dev/cu.usbserial-XXXX --baud 115200 --before no-reset --after no-reset-stub verify-flash 0x0 petkit-stock-a.bin

			
Write at 0x0, not 0x10000: the dump is the whole flash.

		

	


	

		
10. Troubleshooting

		

			
SymptomFix

			
Failed to connect, no answerWrong header (use the one next to the ESP32), TX and RX swapped, or the device isn't in download mode: hold OK while plugging the power in.

			
Download mode never startsThe board must be powered by its own power adapter, not by the serial adapter.

			
Old advice says to hold a button on GPIO2That's wrong for the T4: GPIO2 powers the display. Download mode is the OK button, on GPIO0.

			
A transfer stops halfwayLower the speed (step 3), use shorter wires, and make sure the probes don't move.

			
The dump is rejected by the extractorPartial dump (4 MB instead of 8 MB) or corrupted read: read it again.

			
No Wi-Fi after flashingJoin the fallback access point and check the Wi-Fi credentials of secrets.yaml.

			
MCU Link Lost on, or MCU Handshake offThe ESP32 doesn't hear the motor MCU: check the board connectors that were unplugged while opening, then use Reboot MCU.

		

		
Something else? Open an issue on GitHub with your board revision and the device logs.
